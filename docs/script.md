## Voice-over (30 s, ~68 words)

> Meet Sunny! He asked a big question: why is the sky blue? Sunlight looks white,
> but it's a secret rainbow! When it hits the air, it bumps into tiny molecules.
> Blue light bounces the most, scattering everywhere! That's why your eyes see
> blue from every direction. At sunset, blue scatters away, leaving reds and
> oranges. Mystery solved!

## Animation notes

* 1.4 walk cycles per second, contact → down → pass → up, feet driven by IK targets.
* Head turns precede arm gestures by ~4 frames (anticipation).
* Every shot ends on a held pose so the 0.34 s crossfade reads cleanly.
* Captions pop with an overshoot ease 0.4 s after the shot starts — never on top of the face.

## Science notes

Rayleigh scattering: intensity ∝ 1/λ⁴. Violet scatters more than blue, but the eye's
photopic response peaks near 555 nm and ozone absorbs part of the violet, so the sky
reads blue. At sunset the optical path through the atmosphere is much longer, the blue
is scattered out of the direct beam, and reds/oranges dominate.
